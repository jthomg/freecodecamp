# Verification record: Growers and Houses

Everything here is secondary: search excerpts (WebFetch is blocked) plus the Atlas's own data. Where
a figure is computed, the arithmetic is shown. NOTE: the shared web-search budget ran out partway
through research (before ep2's "bottles could burst" and the ageing rules could be searched), so
a few general-knowledge lines are marked as such and should get a second look.

## Ep1 Two Sides of One Wine
- 1/3: growers farm, houses buy grapes or young wine and sell under their own names. Comité Champagne "Champagne at a glance" (https://www.champagne.fr/en/find-out-more/champagne-at-a-glance); UMC Missions page (https://maisons-champagne.com/en/professional/umc/origins-and-mission/article/missions). secondary.
- 1/4: 16,460 growers, 120 cooperatives, 410 houses, 2025 figures. Comité "at a glance" (2025). The 2024 factsheet (champagne.education Fiche Chiffres Clés, 2023 data) gives 16,200 / 130 / 370, which is why card 5 says counts move. secondary.
- 1/6: growers farm "nearly 90%"; houses "slightly more than 10%". Comité "at a glance"; UMC Missions. secondary.
- 1/7: 34,200 ha in production (2025). Comité "at a glance". Computed: ~10% of 34,200 = ~3,400 ha, hence "a few thousand hectares". secondary.
- 1/8: 266.1m bottles shipped in 2025, houses 191.9m. Comité "at a glance"; Drinks Business; Wine Industry Advisor 21 Jan 2026. Computed 191.9/266.1 = 72.1%, "a little over seven in ten". secondary.
- 1/9-10: growers 51.3m, cooperatives 22.9m, houses 191.6m of 265.9m. Sophie Claeys via Meininger (https://www.meininger.de/en/meiningers-international/champagne/champagne-sees-sales-decline). Computed 19.3% and 8.6%. Single trade analysis; card says so. secondary.
- 1/11: houses' share of value "close to four-fifths". Lea & Sandeman blog citing CIVC ("78% of turnover"), year unclear. FLAG (date of figure unknown; card hedged as "quoted in the trade").
- 1/13: most grapes growers harvest are sold to houses. Comité "at a glance" (via excerpt). secondary.
- 1/16: NM = négociant-manipulant, RM = récoltant-manipulant. Label code sources (Lea & Sandeman 2023; Wikipedia Grower Champagne); Atlas label.html. secondary / Atlas data.
- 1/18: "close to three centuries": 1729 to 2026 = 297 years. computed.

- 2/3-4: 1 September 1729 charter in his account book, Reims; first wines as gifts to the family's cloth customers; cloth dropped c.1735 ("within a few years"). Wikipedia Ruinart / Nicolas Irénée Ruinart; Atlas maison_founding.js. secondary / Atlas data.
- 2/5: "oldest established champagne house". Wikipedia Ruinart; pepites-en-champagne; Ruinart's own framing. secondary.
- 2/6: Gosset 1584, Aÿ, still mostly red wine. Wikipedia Gosset; Wilson Daniels (importer); champagner.com glossary (notes Stevenson's 1555 Jean Gosset mention). Atlas maison_founding.js 1584. secondary. (1555 kept off.)
- 2/8: Moët 1743, Claude Moët, Épernay, wine trader; Clicquot 1772, Philippe Clicquot, Reims. Multiple secondary; Atlas maison_founding.js. secondary.
- 2/9: Heidsieck 1785, cloth merchant from Westphalia; "first of the German founders". Wikipedia Florens-Louis Heidsieck; IdealWine; schiller-wine. Atlas says "draper", consistent. secondary.
- 2/10: Mumm 1827, brothers, sons of a German wine merchant (sources also say banker); Bollinger 1829, Aÿ, German-born partner. Wikipedia Bollinger; VinePair; Atlas maison_founding.js (which names Joseph Bollinger while search gives Jacob; first name kept off). secondary.
- 2/11: Deutz 1838 Aÿ, two Germans; Krug 1843, Mainz-born, bookkeeper, worked at Jacquesson. Wikipedia Champagne Krug; wein.plus Deutz; Atlas. secondary.
- 2/13: some German newcomers were sons of winegrowers. IdealWine (https://www.idealwine.info/heidsieck-krug-bollinger-how-germany-shaped-champagne/). secondary.
- 2/14, 2/17-20: the capital / cellar / customers argument. Interpretation, supported in outline by Asia Tatler (houses held capital and buyer relationships) and UMC. FLAG as interpretation; no specific claims.
- 2/15: 15 months minimum, 3 years vintage. Atlas process.html; general knowledge of the AOC rules. Atlas data (search budget exhausted before a separate check).
- 2/16: bottles could burst in early cellars. general knowledge (no number given). FLAG: unsearched.
- 2/18: buyers in Paris and London. general knowledge (no specific claim).

## Ep3 The Growers' Long Wait
- 3/3-4: growers making own champagne from the early 19th c., very unusual, mostly around Épernay; a Boursault grower in 1876. Comité "Champagne Winegrowers" page (https://www.champagne.fr/en/find-out-more/champagne-industry/the-champagne-growers) via a merged excerpt. FLAG: the Boursault/1876 detail came from a summarised excerpt; confirm the source page.
- 3/5: récoltant-manipulant meaning. Label sources; general French. secondary.
- 3/8: 1911 Marne riots, outside grapes. Approved grand-cru-premier-cru record (HANDOFF round 3). secondary.
- 3/9: riots "often described as" a pressure toward rules on origin. Same record's softened wording. secondary.
- 3/10: Depression, grape prices fell tenfold to under 1 franc/kg, vineyards at risk. UMC "The 1930s" chapter VI (https://maisons-champagne.com/en/encyclopedias/the-great-story-of-champagne-houses/chapter-vi/article/the-1930s-the-trade-association-takes-its-first). One-sided (houses' body); card attributes it. secondary.
- 3/11-13: unions without enforcement; Prefect fixed a minimum price after 1929; Commission de Châlons 1935; grapes below price not approved for champagne. Same UMC source. secondary.
- 3/14: CIVC 1941, now Comité Champagne. Grand-cru record (Wikipedia, Légifrance); UMC "How it began". secondary.
- 3/15: grower bottling quickened in the 1930s. The Whisky Exchange guide (single source; said houses "stopped buying", which UMC does not support, so the card softens it). FLAG.
- 3/16-17: almost all growers sold grapes until mid-20th c.; from the 1950s manipulation developed. Wikipedia Grower Champagne. secondary.

## Ep4 The Cooperatives
- 4/3: cooperatives traced to the early 20th c., era of growers' protests. Épernay tourism site (epernay-tourisme.com cooperatives page). Weak source; card says "often traced". FLAG.
- 4/4-7: Mailly 1929; about two dozen growers (the house's text says 24, then 25, so card says "about two dozen"); houses opposed; no business for two years; rented equipment, recycled bottles, 3,600 bottles to a single buyer. Champagne Mailly "A human adventure" (https://www.champagne-mailly.com/en/human-adventure/); Wikipedia Champagne Mailly. The cooperative's own account; attributed on the card. secondary.
- 4/4: "one of the oldest" (not "the first"). Wikipedia / Mailly. secondary.
- 4/9: cooperatives pressed and stored members' yields. Wikipedia Grower Champagne. secondary.
- 4/10: Le Mesnil cooperative 1937; Union Champagne, Avize, 1966. Drinks Business 2007 "Champagne cooperatives: working together". secondary.
- 4/11-12: Centre Vinicole 1971 or 1972 (sources differ, stated on card); Nicolas Feuillatte brand created 1976, bought 1986. Wikipedia Champagne Nicolas Feuillatte; Drinks Business 2007. secondary.
- 4/13-16: CM, RC, RM, MA definitions. Lea & Sandeman 2023; enviedechamp; glassofbubbly; Atlas label.html. secondary / Atlas data.
- 4/17: 120 cooperatives (Comité 2025); 22.9m bottles (Claeys via Meininger); 8.6% computed. secondary.

## Ep7 The Grandes Marques and Today's Market
- 7/1: 1882, thirty-five houses, Syndicat du Commerce des Vins de Champagne, to protect the name. UMC "How it began" (https://maisons-champagne.com/en/professional/umc/origins-and-mission/article/how-it-began). Pernod Ricard says four founders; card follows the UMC. secondary.
- 7/2: 1887 ruling, confirmed by the Cour de Cassation 1889. UMC "How it began". secondary.
- 7/4-5: before 1994 a federation of Syndicat de Grandes Marques and Syndicat des Négociants; since 1994 houses belong directly to the UMC. UMC Missions page. Disbanded 1997: VinePair; wines.com forum. secondary.
- 7/6: ended over failure to agree quality criteria. grapeescapes.net only. FLAG (card says single account).
- 7/7: no settled definition. Champagne Club ("no certain definition"). secondary.
- 7/9-11: 1959 contrat interprofessionnel; price as % of bottle price: 28, 32, 36, 36, 34 across editions; CIVC set price and shared out crop; five editions over three decades; sixth edition dropped in 1990, return to individual contracts. UMC "How winegrowers and houses do business together" and chapter IX. secondary.
- 7/12: contract removed incentive for quality. UMC chapter IX. secondary.
- 7/13: no agreed price, which EU rules bar. Harpers (2004 trade agreement); grand-cru record (1990, EU competition pressure). secondary.
- 7/14: multi-year contracts common. Harpers 2004 (five-year term then). Current terms not confirmed; card says "have long been common". FLAG mild.
- 7/15: grapes about 36% of average bottle price, stable over a decade. UMC (via Drinks Business 2023 search excerpt). secondary.
- 7/16-17: 2025 rendement 9,000 kg/ha, about 1,000 below 2024, to slow stock build-up. Vinetur; Réussir; Journal du Luxe. secondary.
- 7/18: LVMH leading group; Dom Pérignon, Krug, Moët & Chandon, Mercier, Veuve Clicquot. Lea & Sandeman "Champagne: the jigsaw of the market". "among others" covers e.g. Ruinart. secondary.
- 7/19: 2025 Vranken-Pommery agreed sale of Heidsieck & Co Monopole to Lanson-BCC. Drinks Business Sept/Oct 2025; Vitisphere; luxus-plus. Price kept off (EUR 50m vs "over 100 million"). secondary.
