## Ep1 What Makes Champagne Champagne
- e1/c2. 34,200 ha and 319 villages (2024). Source: Comité Champagne key-figures factsheet 2024 (champagne.education/sites/default/files/2024-05/Factsheet-Key-Figures-Champagne-EN.pdf). UMC gives 34,300 ha; guide.html gives 319. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- e1/c4-5. Seven permitted varieties, the main three almost all of the vines. Sources: Decanter "Champagne grapes"; wineberserkers (2010 rules); guide.html ("heritage varieties under 0.3% of plantings"). Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- e1/c6. The Comité/interprofession sets the yield each year. Source: HANDOFF round-3 record (AAWE paper, Wikipedia: "manages harvest size"). Not re-searched (budget). Status: secondary, FLAG (light).
- e1/c7. Whole bunches, so hand-picking in practice. Sources: UMC "Pressing" (whole clusters "rules out mechanical harvesting"); process.html ("the law allows nothing else"). The card stays with the UMC framing. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- e1/c8-9. 102 L per 160 kg; cuvée then taille (20.5 hl + 5 hl from a 4,000 kg marc). Sources: UMC Pressing; champagne.education harvest page; Wikipedia "Cuvée". Status: secondary.
- e1/c12. About 6 bar at 20°C. Source: Comité Champagne factsheet "Champagne Making Process" (champagne.education PDF 2024-05). Krug says 6-8, so the card says "around six". Status: secondary.
- e1/c14. Non-vintage: at least 15 months from tirage, at least 12 on the lees. Sources: alafrancaise.fr vocabulary; winewithseth (vintage vs NV). Some sites say "15 months on lees". process.html says "fifteen months". No INAO text was reached. Status: secondary, FLAG (confirm the 12-month lees split in the cahier des charges).
- e1/c15. Vintage: at least 3 years from bottling; most wait longer. Sources: winewithseth comparison page; Taittinger "vintage vs non-vintage"; UMC ("most winemakers ... much longer"). I deliberately did not write "three years on the lees" (school.html's wording). Status: secondary.
- e1/c13, c16. Lees, autolysis, remuage, dégorgement, dosage, liqueur d'expédition. Definitions only. Status: general knowledge.
- e1/c17-18. The name is protected in the EU and in many other markets. No count of countries and no AOC year are given. Not separately searched (budget). Status: general knowledge.

## Ep2 Crémant
- e2/c1. Crémant reserved for AOC wines by French law no. 75-577 of 4 July 1975, and later at EU level (EEC 1992). Source: effervescents-du-monde.com crémant page. Single source. Status: secondary, FLAG (light).
- e2/c2. Eight appellations, named; Savoie the youngest, approved by the INAO in 2014. Sources: Decanter "French crémant sparkling wines"; agra.fr ("les huit appellations"). Glass of Bubbly says "seven", but lists eight. Status: secondary.
- e2/c5. Hand harvest; first 100 L per 150 kg. Sources: winewithseth crémant production; glassofbubbly. Status: secondary.
- e2/c6. Arithmetic: 100/150 = 0.667 and 102/160 = 0.6375. Status: computed.
- e2/c7. At least 9 months on lees (Champagne 12). Loire releases at 12 months from tirage; Bordeaux needs 12 months in total before disgorgement. Sources: winewithseth (Loire and Bordeaux AOC pages). Status: secondary.
- e2/c8. Loire at least 4 bar and Bordeaux at least 3.5 bar, both at 20°C. Source: winewithseth Loire and Bordeaux pages. Status: secondary.
- e2/c9. Lower pressure "often described as" softer. Status: general knowledge, hedged.
- e2/c12-14. Reg. (EEC) 2333/92 allowed the use of "méthode champenoise" only where traditional, for five wine years from 1 Sept 1989 (so ending 1994). The CJEU in C-306/93 (13 Dec 1994) found nothing affecting its validity. Sources: search excerpt citing the regulation and the judgment; vinsvignesvignerons (3 Aug 1994); cellars.com.au. The card keeps the court's role narrow, as the judgment did. Status: secondary.
- e2/c16. Local equivalent terms (método tradicional, metodo classico). Sources: Wikipedia "Traditional method"; winewithseth. Status: secondary.
- e2/c17. Reg. (EU) 2019/33: "traditional method" requires a second fermentation in bottle and at least 9 months on lees. Source: search excerpt (cellars.com.au / Wikipedia). Status: secondary.
- e2/c18. Crémant de Luxembourg, from a 1991 government regulation. Sources: Wikipedia "Crémant de Luxembourg"; gov.uk protected names. I deliberately did NOT say "the only country outside France" (one glossary claims Belgian and German crémants). Status: secondary.

- e4/c3-4. Autoclave and the process. Sources: winewithseth; puninwine. Status: secondary.

- e4/c12. Conegliano Valdobbiadene and Asolo DOCG, "Prosecco Superiore". Sources: Wine Enthusiast; tastings.com. Asolo was formerly Colli Asolani. Status: secondary.

- e4/c15. Frizzante at no more than 2.5 bar; spumante and tranquillo. Source: VinePair / mywinepal. The spumante threshold (3 vs 3.5 bar) conflicts and is left off. Status: secondary.

- e4/c17. Col Fondo, fermented in the bottle; a niche. Source: Wine Enthusiast / tastings excerpt. Status: secondary.

## Ep6 English Sparkling Wine
- e6/c1. Sparkling is about two-thirds of production (68%). Source: WineGB-based report (Decanter "74% over five years" / terroirsense). Status: secondary.
- e6/c2-3. Chardonnay 31%, Pinot Noir 29%, Meunier 9%, Bacchus 8%. Source: WineGB-based summaries (lunzerwine 2026; winedrinker WineGB report). Not the primary PDF. Status: secondary, FLAG (light).
- e6/c4. July 2024: 4,209 ha, from government data, 123% in 10 years. Sources: WineGB 2024-07-03 press release; wineanorak 2024-07-05. Status: secondary (trade body).
- e6/c5. "More than doubled": 123% growth means 2.23 times. Latest figure 4,840.91 ha, citing Wine Standards 2025. Source: WineGB Data page (excerpt). Single source. Status: secondary, FLAG (light).
- e6/c6. Arithmetic: 4,841 / 34,200 = 0.14, roughly one-seventh. Status: computed.
- e6/c7. Over 1,000 vineyards in 2023. Source: WineGB 2024-07-03. Status: secondary.
- e6/c9-11. The Anglo-Paris Basin; Late Cretaceous white chalk; the same broad deposit, but not identical bed for bed. Sources: ResearchGate "Chalk of the Anglo-Paris Basin"; Paris Basin clay-mineralogy paper; winewithseth Southeast England chalk ("differ in stratigraphic detail"). That the North and South Downs are chalk is general knowledge. Status: secondary, FLAG (no stratigraphy source correlating the formations directly).
- e6/c12. Many English vineyards grow on sands and clays. Not searched (budget). Status: general knowledge, FLAG.
- e6/c13. Chalk drains freely and holds water. Sources: winewithseth chalk soils; enviedechamp (chalk as a water reservoir). Status: secondary.
- e6/c14-15. Taittinger with Hatch Mansfield; announced 2015; Chilham, Kent, on chalk; planted 2017. Classic Cuvée presented to the trade in Sept 2024; public release "planned" for March 2025. Sources: wineanorak 2025-01-28; The Buyer (Sept 2024); Restaurant Online 2017-05-04; Decanter. The land purchase is given as 2014 or 2015, so no purchase date is on the card. Status: secondary.
- e6/c16-17. Vranken-Pommery joint venture with Hattingley Valley, 2014; Louis Pommery England; Pinglestone near Alresford; made at Hattingley's winery. Sources: Wine Alchemy; CB Insights; search excerpts. I avoided "owns" and wrote "invested in a vineyard site". Status: secondary.

## Ep7 A Warming Champagne
- e7/c2. +1.1°C over 30 years, attributed to the Comité. Sources: IWC "Are warmer harvests causing problems"; ESG News. Status: secondary.
- e7/c3. About 10 days over three decades (INRAE scientists, via Reuters). Sources: Japan Times 2026-08-18; Yahoo/Reuters syndication. Status: secondary.
- e7/c4. About 18 days earlier, attributed to the Comité in the trade press. Sources: IWC; enviedechamp. NB: an Assemblée nationale question gives "18 days over 40 years" for France generally (INRAE/ONERC). The card states the range rather than picking one figure. Status: secondary, FLAG (the Comité's original source was not found).
- e7/c6. 2026 opening dates 8-22 August; the co-president called the ban "la plus précoce jamais observée". Sources: Boursorama 2026-08-12; Agri Mutuel. The Reuters coverage gives other dates (12 Aug; 22 Aug), but 8-22 is the published span by commune and grape. The person is not named. Status: secondary.
- e7/c7-8. Potential alcohol +0.7% vol and acidity -1.3 g/L over 30 years. Source: trade press citing the CIVC (IWC / VinePair excerpt). Not the primary dataset. Status: secondary, FLAG.
- e7/c9-10. The INAO and the Comité allowed a temporary exception for the 2026 harvest, up to 15%, above the usual ceiling (reported as 13%, kept off the card), affecting a small share of producers. Average potential alcohol stayed below 12%. Sources: vinetur 2026-09-14; Nation Thailand; The Drinks Business 2026/09; napavalleywineacademy. Status: secondary.
- e7/c11-13. Voltis: resistant to mildew and powdery mildew; INAO trial approval (10 Feb meeting, 2022; guide.html says "experimental since 2022"); up to 5% of area and 10% of a blend; a 10-year trial; aimed at cutting spraying. Sources: wein.plus; PIWI International 2022-02; vinetur 2026-04 ("to cut spraying"). Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- e7/c14. Arbane and Petit Meslier keep their acidity in warm years. Sources: rebeccagibb.com; solardaily; tysonstelzer. Status: secondary.
- e7/c15. Vignes semi-larges approved, lower density, acidity and frost. Source: enviedechamp / tastefrance excerpt. Status: secondary.
- e7/c16. The Qanopée greenhouse, with Burgundy and Beaujolais, for new clones and rootstocks. Source: enviedechamp "Climate change: how is Champagne adapting?". Status: secondary.
- e7/c17. Taller canopies, cover crops. Source: search excerpt (VinePair / tastefrance). Status: secondary.
- e7/c18. A ban on irrigating adult vines. Source: search excerpt (tastefrance). Status: secondary.
- e7/c19-20. Opinion divided on style. Sources: The Buyer "Warmer, riper, different Champagne"; vinetur (producers worried about higher alcohol). Status: secondary, hedged.
