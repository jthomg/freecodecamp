# The Grapes: verification record

All of this is secondary: search excerpts (WebFetch is blocked) plus the Atlas's own data. Every
number below is somewhere in the cards. The search budget ran out before two last checks
(Chardonnay's parentage, a Comité pesticide target), so both were left off the cards.

## Ep1: Three Grapes, and How They Came to Dominate
- **c1-3: the rulebook lists seven principal varieties; Chardonnay Rose added 2025; Voltis on trial.** The cahier des charges published in the BO on 15 Dec 2022 (sgv-champagne.fr/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/Cahier-des-charges-AOC-Champagne.pdf) lists the seven and names Voltis as "variété d'intérêt à fin d'adaptation". Chardonnay Rose: champagne.fr press release (champagne.fr/system/files/2025-10/...Chardonnay Rose.pdf), Vitisphere and Harpers. Homologated by the arrêté of 31 July 2025 (JORF 5 Aug 2025). Status: secondary (excerpts of primary documents).
- **c5-6: 2023 vineyard 38% PN / 31% CH / 30% M / others <1%.** SGV Champagne (the growers' union) "Pocket 2024" and "Propositions 2024", labelled "du vignoble 2023". The DRAAF Grand Est May 2021 sheet citing the Comité gives 38/31/31, and champagne.fr's undated grape page gives 38/31/31. Other sites give 38/32/30. The card names the year and the publisher, and says the sources round differently. Status: secondary. NOTE: this is the growers' union, NOT a Comité Champagne publication for 2023; no Comité 2023+ breakdown was found.
- **c9: phylloxera first noticed 6 Aug 1890 at Tréloup(-Chassaing), Aisne.** UMC (maisons-champagne.com, Phylloxera article) and techno-science. Taittinger says 1890 at "Trélou-sur-Marne"; a Substack says 1891. The card follows UMC. Status: secondary.
- **c10: grafting onto American rootstock.** UMC and general knowledge.
- **c11-12: replanting favoured the productive three; old grapes survived mainly in the south.** Club Oenologique ("post-phylloxera and post-war replanting favoured quantity"); Wikipedia (Arbane, around Bar-sur-Aube). Status: secondary, causal claim softened ("tended to").
- **c13: Aube admitted 1927 on condition of replacing Gamay.** fr.wikipedia Champagne (AOC) ("60 communes ... à condition de remplacer le gamay"). Status: secondary. The end date is kept off (see below).
- **c14: Meunier about half the vineyard mid-twentieth century.** Club Oenologique "Meunier rising" and everydaydrinking.com. Phrased "several accounts suggest". FLAG: no official series found.
- **c15: Chardonnay planted more widely in the following decades / blanc de blancs demand.** Fizzyvines (single source). Phrased "is said to". FLAG.
- **c16: proportions stable since the 1980s, CH up, M down.** champagne-guide.fr (single source). The card names it as "one French guide". Status: secondary.

## Ep2: Pinot Noir
- **c1-2: pigment (anthocyanins) in the skins; colourless pulp.** General knowledge.
- **c3: whole bunches, pressed immediately after picking, slowly.** champagne.fr "The Champagne-making process" fact sheet (2022-10 PDF). Status: secondary (excerpt of official doc).
- **c4-5: marc 4,000 kg, max 2,550 L; cuvée 2,050 L, taille 500 L; taille more coloured and less balanced.** alafrancaise.fr glossary; Champagne Jean Comyn PDF; Champagne Auge pressing page. One producer site gives 20 hl/5.5 hl. The card follows the 2,050/500 majority. Status: secondary.
- **c7: PN is the main grape in Bouzy, Ambonnay and Verzenay.** village_grapes.js: Bouzy 87%, Ambonnay 81%, Verzenay 86% PN. Status: Atlas data.
- **c8: Verzenay on the northern flank.** Approved grand-cru thread ep4 / villages.html. Status: Atlas data.
- **c9: Grande Vallée around Aÿ is a PN stronghold.** guide.html (Grande Vallée de la Marne, 65% PN); village_grapes.js Aÿ 89% PN. Status: Atlas data.
- **c10: Côte des Bar in the far south, "not far from Burgundy".** BKWine ("140 km from Epernay and not far from Burgundy"). Status: secondary.
- **c11: PN share above 80%; Les Riceys almost only PN.** Sources give 80-86% (Club Oenologique, World of Fine Wine, Wine Scholar Guild, Vinovonk). village_grapes.js: Les Riceys 92% PN. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- **c12: Kimmeridgian marl, shared with Chablis.** Club Oenologique, Vinovonk, Wine Scholar Guild. Status: secondary.
- **c13: PN buds early, frost risk.** WSET Global blog 2025. Status: secondary.
- **c14: thin skins, tight bunches, rot risk.** General knowledge; WSET on rainfall and rot.
- **c15: given the warmer sites.** Matthew's World of Wine / WSET Diploma notes ("reserved for the warmest sites"). Status: secondary.

## Ep3: Chardonnay and the Côte des Blancs
- **c1-2: Cramant, Avize, Oger and Le Mesnil 100% CH; Chouilly 99, Oiry 99.** village_grapes.js. Status: Atlas data.
- **c3: mostly east-facing; morning sun helps against frost.** winewithseth, winetourism. Status: secondary.
- **c4-5: belemnite chalk; drains, yet holds water for dry summers.** Larmandier-Bernier (geology page), lebendigeweine, Wikipedia. The litres-per-cubic-metre figure (300-400) is kept off. Status: secondary.
- **c8: CH buds early; heavy frost losses on the Côte des Blancs.** WSET; decanter (2008: CdB average losses of 74% per one report, not on a card). Status: secondary.
- **c9: April 2021: one large house estimated higher bud loss in CH than in PN or M.** The Drinks Business, May 2021 (Moët & Chandon: CH 20-25%, PN 15%, M 10%; an early estimate). No numbers are on the card. Status: secondary.
- **c10: Vertus mostly CH with a little PN.** village_grapes.js 91/9. Status: Atlas data.
- **c11: Sézanne a good deal of CH; Montgueux near Troyes known for CH.** village_grapes.js (Sézanne 71% CH, Montgueux 91% CH). Status: Atlas data. "Chalky hill" is general knowledge.
- **c13: blanc de blancs = white grapes only, usually all CH.** Comité label guide (champagne.fr "How to read a Champagne label" PDF); wine-searcher. Status: secondary.
- **c16: "Chardonnay ages most gracefully"** is phrased as a widely held opinion (school.html states it as fact).
- **c17: six of the 17 grand crus are here.** Approved grand-cru thread. Status: Atlas data.

## Ep4: Meunier
- **c1: name from "miller"; downy white leaf undersides.** BBR, Wikipedia, winetraveler. Status: secondary.
- **c2: a close relative of PN, formerly "Pinot Meunier"; the region's bodies (CIVC, INRA) encourage "Meunier".** Wine-Searcher. Whether it is a PN mutation or a distinct variety is disputed, so the card says only "close relative". Status: secondary.
- **c3-4: Vallée de la Marne heartland; Festigny 86% M; the Rive Gauche is the most Meunier-dominated sector.** village_grapes.js; guide.html (Rive Gauche 75% M). Festigny's left-bank position is general knowledge. Status: Atlas data.
- **c5: frost gathers on low ground.** General knowledge; lebendigeweine ("low-lying area that gets more frost").
- **c6-7: buds later than PN and CH; ripens early (VERIFIED, several sources).** WSET 2025 blog, Vinovonk lexicon, everydaydrinking. Status: secondary, consistent.
- **c8: clay soils.** WSET, glassofbubbly. Status: secondary.
- **c10-11: workhorse reputation; "does not age" called an opinion.** Wine Enthusiast, Meininger's, everydaydrinking. Status: secondary.
- **c12: Krug's blends include a significant share of Meunier and age well.** Club Oenologique / cellartours (20-25%, kept off the card). Status: secondary.
- **c13: José Michel; La Closerie, Jérôme Prévost, Gueux.** winetraditions (Peter Liem), everydaydrinking, decanter. Status: secondary. A public producer, named because it is essential to the "champions" beat; School already names Prévost.

## Ep5: The Other Four
- **c2: together well under 1%.** SGV ("autres <1%"). The champagne.fr Chardonnay Rose release says 0.5% for the minor varieties; others say 0.3% / ~90-93 ha. The card gives no exact figure. Status: secondary.
- **c3: Pinot Blanc the largest of the four "in most accounts"; especially in the Aube.** cluboenologique (82 ha), others (~100 ha); Wine Scholar Guild (Côte des Bar). Status: secondary.
- **c4: Pinot Gris also called Fromenteau.** Wikipedia "Fromenteau" ("historical documents from Champagne repeatedly use the name Fromenteau for Pinot Gris"); Laherte's Les 7 lists "Fromenteau". VERIFIED (secondary).
- **c5: Petit Meslier = Gouais Blanc x Savagnin per DNA; high acidity.** Wikipedia Petit Meslier; wein.plus glossary; plantgrape. Status: secondary.
- **c6: Arbane the rarest, around Bar-sur-Aube.** Wikipedia Arbane. Status: secondary.
- **c7: Arbane area estimates range from under 1 ha to over 10 ha.** jjbuckley (<1 ha); others give 5, 12 and 15 ha. The card states the range, NOT "nearly extinct". Status: secondary, conflicting, stated as such.
- **c9: Petit Meslier buds early, coulure/millerandage; Arbane ripening, frost and disease.** plantgrape; wein.plus. Status: secondary.
- **c11: Moutard (Buxeuil, Côte des Bar): pure Arbane; Cuvée Six Cépages uses six of the seven.** champagneeveryday, decanter; growers.js. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- **c12: Drappier Quattuor = Arbane, Petit Meslier, Pinot Blanc, Chardonnay; Urville, Côte des Bar.** champagneeveryday; growers.js. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- **c13: Aubry grows all seven; Tarlant (Œuilly, Marne valley); Laherte Frères (Chavot, near Épernay), Les 7.** Grape Collective, cluboenologique, lebendigeweine; growers.js for the villages. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- **c16-17: Chardonnay Rose a natural mutation with pigmented skins, added 2025, catalogued 2018, certified plantings expected 2027.** champagne.fr press release, Vitisphere, Harpers, iDealwine. Status: secondary (official press release via excerpt).

## Ep6: Voltis
- **Hook: "reported to be the first resistant variety in a French appellation".** wein.plus headline. Status: secondary, attributed.
- **c2: downy and powdery mildew.** General knowledge; PIWI International.
- **c5: bred by INRAE and the Julius Kühn Institute, programme begun 2000.** Loki Wine, champagneeveryday, thirtyfifty. Status: secondary. FLAG: some excerpts garble the French institute's name.
- **c6: fully resistant to powdery, highly resistant to downy mildew.** thirtyfifty / PIWI excerpt. Status: secondary.
- **c7: ancestry includes American species (V. berlandieri, V. rupestris, Muscadinia).** champagneeveryday. Status: secondary.
- **c8: proposed 2021; INAO approval Feb 2022; rules published end 2022.** fromthevinetowine (summer 2021), PIWI International (Feb 2022), BO 15 Dec 2022 cahier. The "end of 2022" that some sources give for approval is reconciled as the publication. Status: secondary.
- **c9: VIFA status.** Cahier 2022; La Champagne Viticole. Status: secondary.
- **c10: 5% of the holding, 10% of the blend (VERIFIED, consistent).** PIWI International, Vitisphere, levigneronchampenois.comitechampagne.fr. Status: secondary.
- **c11: must be blended; the name may not appear on the label.** levigneronchampenois.comitechampagne.fr "Vinifier le Voltis en appellation Champagne". Status: secondary (Comité grower guidance via excerpt).
- **c12: first plantings 2023, first real harvest 2025; small area.** Vitisphere (both), jancisrobinson. Status: secondary.
- **c13: decision in the early 2030s; sources differ.** Vitisphere ("after 2032"), wein.plus ("2033"). PIWI says 5 years renewable once, others 10 years. Stated as a disagreement.
- **c14: harvests ~20 days earlier than 30 years ago, per the Comité.** Japan Times, 18 Aug 2026, citing the Comité. An older CIVC-based piece says 18 days. Status: secondary.
- **c15: 2026 record-early harvest, picking from 12 August.** Japan Times 2026-08-18; Fortune 2026-08-20 (opening 12-17 Aug by village). Status: secondary.
- **c16: more sugar, less acidity.** International Wine Challenge article (the figures are kept off). Status: secondary.
