# Champagne Understood — verification record

All research is secondary: web search excerpts (WebFetch is blocked), plus the Atlas's own data
files. No primary archive was consulted. Statuses: **secondary** (search excerpt(s) as named),
**Atlas data** (file named), **general knowledge** (no specific number, name or date), **FLAG**
(thin, single or conflicting source; confirm before shipping).

Note: the session's web-search budget ran out before every lookup I planned was done. One
planned check (pre-riddling clearing methods, such as transvasage) was not run, so that claim was
taken off the cards.

## Ep1 The Monk Who Fought the Bubbles

- 1.1 Benedictine, from Sainte-Menehould; only the baptism record survives, dated 5 January 1639. GuildSomm (Stevenson, "Dom Pérignon: Fact & Fiction") via excerpt; History Hit; Wikipedia "Dom Pérignon (monk)". Others give 1638 as the birth year; the card says only the baptism date. secondary
- 1.2 Sent to Hautvillers in 1668 and stayed for life. Wikipedia; GuildSomm; UMC "Abbaye d'Hautvillers". secondary
- 1.3 Procureur (steward) and cellar master, second only to the abbot. GuildSomm (translated mirror excerpt); rfgenealogie.com (procureur = intendant). secondary
- 1.4 Died 1715; "nearly half a century" (1668 to 1715 = 47 years, computed). Most sources give 24 September 1715, Find a Grave gives 14 September, so the card gives the year only. secondary
- 1.5 Bubbles regarded as a fault. The Conversation (Garritt Van Dyk, 2023). secondary
- 1.6 Spring refermentation after a cold-halted fermentation; weak French bottles. The Conversation and others (weak French glass); the mechanism is general knowledge. general knowledge
- 1.7 His effort went into keeping wine still. The Conversation ("he was trying to get rid of the bubbles"). secondary
- 1.8 to 1.9 White wine from black grapes (Pinot Noir), no treading, several presses. Wikipedia "Dom Pérignon (monk)" via excerpt; rarewineinvest. secondary
- 1.10 Blanc de noirs definition. general knowledge
- 1.11 Did not introduce blending, but blended grapes before pressing. Wikipedia "Dom Pérignon (monk)" excerpt. One blog calls him "father of assemblage"; the card follows the more careful version. secondary
- 1.12 to 1.13 Assemblage definition; non-vintage blends rely on multiple sources. general knowledge
- 1.14 to 1.16 Dom Grossard's letter, 1821 (25 October 1821, to the deputy mayor of Aÿ); a motive to boost the abbey; no earlier document calls Pérignon an inventor. The Conversation; UMC FR "Dom Pérignon, moine bénédictin"; labexhastec.ephe.psl.eu project PDF; search synthesis. 1821 - 1715 = 106 years ("more than a century", computed). secondary
- 1.17 1889 Exposition Universelle pamphlet by the Syndicat du Commerce des Vins de Champagne. The Conversation; todayifoundout; synthesis of several. secondary
- 1.18 "Tasting the stars" line first in print advertising, late nineteenth century. Wikipedia; Historic Mysteries; Fattorini. London Wine Cellar says the 1900s, so the card says "most sources date". secondary, with the disagreement noted
- 1.19 Moët & Chandon put the Dom Pérignon cuvée on sale in 1936. Cavesa/vinovonk excerpt; `maison_founding.js` ("put on sale in 1936"). secondary + Atlas data
