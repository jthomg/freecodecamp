# Verification record: how-champagne-is-made

All of this is secondary: WebSearch excerpts (WebFetch blocked) plus the Atlas's own data. Primary
legal texts were seen only as search excerpts. Status key: secondary / Atlas data / general
knowledge / FLAG.

## Episode 1, A Still Wine First
- e1c2: Pinot Noir and Meunier are black-skinned, two of the three main grapes. Source: general knowledge; process.html. Status: general knowledge.
- e1c3: black grapes have pale flesh; colour (anthocyanins) is in the skins. Sources: mcgill.ca/oss "what does blanc de noirs mean"; winefolly.com. Status: secondary.
- e1c5: hand picking, so bunches reach the press whole and intact. Sources: DGCCRF "Champagne, connaître le langage des étiquettes" (economie.gouv.fr); champagne.education/en/harvest-pressing-champagne. Status: secondary (official bodies, via excerpt). The cards do NOT say "the law forbids machines"; process.html says that, but I did not see the cahier clause. FLAG for SITE if that wording is kept on process.html.
- e1c6: machines shake vines and can split berries. Sources: winewithseth "hand vs machine harvesting"; connexionfrance.com. Status: secondary.
- e1c7-8: whole bunches; pressing soon after picking, slow and fractional. Source: Comité Champagne "Fiche Elaboration Champagne" PDF (champagne.education), "Aussitôt après la cueillette, grappes entières, lent et fractionné". Status: secondary (official teaching material).
- e1c9: marc = 4,000 kg. Sources: veuveclicquot.com/en-us/vinetowine.html; Wikipedia "Cuvée"; process.html. Status: secondary + Atlas data.
- e1c10-11: max 2,550 L per marc; cuvée 2,050 L, taille 500 L. Sources: Veuve Clicquot vine-to-wine page; Wikipedia "Cuvée"; champagner.com glossary "taille"; u-picardie course page. Arithmetic 2,050 + 500 = 2,550. Status: secondary. Note: process.html writes these as "20.5 hL" and "5", and 25.5 hL; consistent.
- e1c12-13: cuvée finer and more acidic; taille fuller, more colour and tannin. Source: lebendigeweine.de; vinovonk.com. Status: secondary.
- e1c14: some producers use only the cuvée. Source: Veuve Clicquot ("generally uses only the cuvée"); internationalfinewines.com.au (Bérêche dropping the taille from Brut Réserve). Status: secondary. Card says "for certain wines", not naming houses.
- e1c16: vin clair. Source: general knowledge; Atlas lexicon. Status: general knowledge.
- e1c18: malolactic optional, some producers block it. Source: process.html; general knowledge. Status: Atlas data / general knowledge.

## Episode 2, Blending Before the Bubbles
- e2c1-2: assemblage of still wines before tirage; INAO doc: chef de cave builds blends from base wines "avant la mise en bouteille pour la prise de mousse". Source: inao.gouv.fr node/32215. Status: secondary.
- e2c6-11: reserve wines for consistency and volume in weak years. Sources: canard-duchene.fr "vins réserve champagne"; thedrinksbusiness.com 2025/02 "Champagne's secret to consistency"; Decanter NV guide. Status: secondary.
- e2c12: reserve share varies widely; higher not automatically better. Source: issuu "Explore Champagne" excerpt (up to 50%; Laurent-Perrier ~10%). No number on any card. Status: secondary.
- e2c14-16: perpetual reserve, partial draw and top-up, like but simpler than solera, fewer larger containers. Sources: gamberorossointernational.com "Will the perpetual reserve save Champagne"; NYT article (mmdusa.net PDF); Drinks Business 2025. Status: secondary (well sourced, as the brief required). The Roederer quotation was deliberately not used (living person).
- e2c17-18: vintage from a single year; still blended across grapes and villages. Status: general knowledge.

## Episode 3, The Second Fermentation
- e3c2-3: liqueur de tirage = wine, sugar, selected yeast, nutrients, riddling aid such as bentonite; bentonite helps the lees slide. Sources: Wikipedia "Liqueur de tirage"; champagnegallery.com.au; winewithseth "tirage liqueur". Status: secondary.
- e3c4: crown cap now usual; bidule catches the lees. Sources: puninwine glossary "crown cap"; champagner.com "bouchage". Status: secondary.
- e3c5: some producers returning to tirage under cork; bottle-to-bottle variation. Source: thedrinksbusiness.com 2024/08 "Is tirage under cork making a comeback". Status: secondary.
- e3c7: prise de mousse, CO2 dissolves. Source: champagne.education factsheet; process.html. Status: secondary.
- e3c8: ~24 g/L tirage sugar; ~4 g/L per atmosphere rule of thumb. Sources: puninwine glossary; UMC tirage page (24 g/L, 6 kg/cm2). Status: secondary.
- e3c9: ~6 bar at 20 °C. Source: Comité Champagne factsheet "Factsheet-Champagne-Making-Process-EN.pdf" (champagne.education). Arithmetic 24/4 = 6. Status: secondary (official). Atlas process.html says "about six atmospheres"; consistent within unit tolerance.
- e3c10: bar ≈ atmosphere; some producers aim lower. Sources: general knowledge (1 atm = 1.013 bar); punchdrink.com "How bubbly should champagne be" (Doyard at 4-5 atm). Status: secondary.
- e3c12: gas equal to 5-6 times bottle volume at ordinary pressure. Sources: diffordsguide "6 fascinating facts" (Liger-Belair); NBC "six bottles' worth". Status: secondary. FLAG (popular-science level; no primary paper seen).
- e3c13-15: early bottles burst; chain breakage; loss figures conflict. Sources: BBR blog "How Champagne got its bubbles"; vinepair; champagne411. Figures given range 4-40% (Redding 1833, via excerpt), 10-80%, 20-90%. Card states the range is wide and picks none. Status: secondary.
- e3c16: stronger English coal-fired glass. Sources: BBR blog; diffordsguide "story and history". Status: secondary. Kept vague on date.
- e3c17-18: 1830s pharmacist in Châlons-sur-Marne, method of measuring sugar; breakage fell; method imprecise. Sources: glossary.wein-plus.eu "François, Jean-Baptiste" and "Réduction François"; lequotidiendupharmacien.fr biography (1792-1838); sur-la-pointe.com (Physiology of Champagne, 1841, 3-8% breakage). Status: secondary. Name not used (see below).
- e3c20: yeast dies, lees; wine dry. Status: general knowledge.

## Episode 4, Waiting on the Lees
- e4c3-7: autolysis; amino acids, proteins, mannoproteins; texture, aroma precursors; lees consume oxygen. Sources: IntechOpen chapter 79500 (Pons-Mercadé et al.); PMC7828459 / Molecules 2021 review. Status: secondary (peer-reviewed reviews, via excerpt).
- e4c8: Oxford Companion: minimal under 18 months, greatest after ~5 years. Source: ckbk.com Oxford Companion entry (excerpt). Status: secondary.
- e4c10: NV at least 15 months from date of tirage before release. Sources: cahier des charges AOC Champagne (sgv-champagne.fr CDC_Champagne_.pdf, homologated 2025; INAO CDCPNOChampagne20240911.pdf), "quinze mois minimum à compter de la date du tirage"; champagne.fr maturation page. Status: secondary (primary text via excerpt).
- e4c11: vintage at least 36 months from date of tirage. Same cahier excerpt. Status: secondary. Note: an older UMC page says "three years after the harvest"; the cahier wording (from tirage) is used.
- e4c12-13: disgorgement not before 12 months from tirage. Source: an OLDER cahier version excerpt ("le dégorgement ne peut être effectué avant une période de douze mois à compter de la date de tirage"); Taittinger and sipchampagnes (12 months on lees). FLAG: the 2025 text excerpt was truncated at this point; confirm the 12-month clause is still current.
- e4c14: typical 2-3 years NV, 4-10 years vintage. Source: champagne.fr maturation page. Status: secondary (official).
- e4c17: disgorgement dates on back labels. Status: general knowledge.

## Episode 5, Turning and Clearing
- e5c2-3: pupitre, two hinged oak boards, 10 rows of 6 holes per side, 120 bottles. Source: UMC "The history of riddling (remuage)". Arithmetic 10 x 6 x 2 = 120. Status: secondary (trade body).
- e5c5: hand riddling six weeks to a few months. Sources: internationalwinechallenge.com "art of riddling" (eight weeks); winewithseth (six weeks to 2-3 months). Card gives the range. Status: secondary.
- e5c6-11: Clicquot story, ~1816, angled holes in a table; UMC tells it as a story with Antoine Müller prompting; dates 1816-1818; legend. Sources: UMC history of riddling; winewithseth "Veuve Clicquot"; millesima-usa; thebubblesreview. Status: secondary. Spelling: UMC "Muller"; others "de Müller", "Antoine-Aloys de Muller". Card uses "Antoine Müller" per the brief. FLAG: confirm preferred spelling.
- e5c11: modern pupitre later. Sources conflict on Michelot patent (1864 or 1889); no date on card. Status: secondary.
- e5c13-14: gyropalette, several hundred bottles (504 cited); patent filed 1968 by two French growers; first sale/adoption 1970s, sources differ (1970 marketing vs prototype 1973 vs programmable 1975; Codorníu mid-1970s; Piper-Heidsieck 1978). Sources: Wikipedia "Gyropalette"; oenoconcept.com; thefinestbubble.com. Status: secondary. All the conflicting dates (1970-1978) fall inside the 1970s, so the card's wording holds whichever is right.
- e5c15: about a week (7-10 days); most champagne riddled by machine. Sources: IWC "art of riddling"; Wikipedia "Gyropalette". Status: secondary.
- e5c17-18: à la volée, still used for some prestige wines and large formats. Sources: canard-duchene.fr "everything you need to know about disgorging"; UMC disgorgement page. Status: secondary.
- e5c19-21: à la glace, brine bath, frozen plug, 1880s, most widely used. Sources: fr.wikipedia "Dégorgement" (Walfard, patents from 1881, Henri Abelé 1884); champagne411; UMC disgorgement page (about -25 °C). Status: secondary. Inventor's name and nationality conflict (Walfart/Walfard; Belgian/French), so no name on the card.

## Episode 6, The Final Touch
- e6c2-4: liqueur d'expédition = wine plus sugar, cane or beet, or RCGM; prepared in advance. Sources: Wikipedia "Dosage (wine)"; UMC dosage page; cavesa.ch. Status: secondary.
- e6c9-10: Maillard reaction post-disgorgement; dosage vs residual sugar role unsettled. Sources: ives-openscience.eu 14080 (IVAS 2022 abstract); Decanter (Tom Hewson, citing Hannah Charnock's research). Status: secondary. Researchers not named on card.
- e6c11-13: agglomerated body, natural discs, mirror; 31 mm; mushroom shape. Sources: GuildSomm (Tom Stevenson) "An overview of champagne corks"; millesima-usa cork article; Wikipedia "Wine cork". Status: secondary.
- e6c14-15: muselet from museler; plaque protects cork; collectable. Sources: Wikipedia "Muselet"; UMC "The history of the muselet and its collectable". Status: secondary.
- e6c16: 1844 patent, Adolphe Jacquesson; coverage disputed (plaque vs wire). Sources: UMC muselet history; Wikipedia; Metinvest PDF. Status: secondary; disagreement stated on card.
- e6c17: six half-turns, counted differently elsewhere. Sources: Wikipedia "Muselet"; worldsteel.org; vinepair. Status: secondary. Note for SITE: process.html says "six half-turns, always six"; sources say "traditionally".
- e6c18-20: bubbles nucleate on cellulose fibres, not glass irregularities; smooth clean glass barely bubbles; microscratches/etching help. Sources: Liger-Belair & Jeandet, Europhysics News 2002 (europhysicsnews.org epn02103.pdf); NBC "physics of fizz". Status: secondary. "Some glasses carry etched marks" = general knowledge.

## Episode 7, Pink Champagne
- e7c3-5: blending red still wine (Pinot Noir and/or Meunier) into white before second fermentation, most common method. Sources: Wikipedia "Rosé"; wineandspiritsmagazine.com ("most producers simply add red wine"). Status: secondary.
- e7c7-9: EU ban on coupage of white and red to make rosé; exemption for blends intended for a cuvée for sparkling wine or for semi-sparkling. Source: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2019/934, Art. 8 (eur-lex consolidated text, via excerpt). Status: secondary (primary text via excerpt). Precision note: the ban as written covers wines without PDO/PGI; PDO rules (e.g. Provence) set their own methods. The card therefore says "ordinary still rosé", and the brief's claim "Champagne is unusual in Europe" is softened to "best-known place where the allowance is used". Champagne is NOT the only region allowed; crémant and other sparkling wines may also use it.
- e7c10: 2009 proposal debated and abandoned. Sources: bkwine.com "How do you make rosé"; lesgrappes.com. Status: secondary.
- e7c12: rosé de saignée / de macération. Status: general knowledge; decanter.com "What does saignée mean".
- e7c15: Drappier makes all four rosés by saignée. Source: thedrinksbusiness.com 2026/06 "Drappier's stand against rosé homogeneity". Status: secondary.
- e7c16: neither method intrinsically better; blending simpler and more repeatable. Source: Wine & Spirits magazine. Status: secondary.
- e7c18: Rosé des Riceys, separate still rosé appellation. Source: INAO CDCPNORoséDesRiceys20240911.pdf; issuu Vine & Bubble. Status: secondary.

## Episode 8, Scorecard
- Restates ep1, ep4, ep5 and ep7 claims; sources as above.
- e8c5: pressing once more generous (2,666 L, third pressing/deuxième taille banned). Source: champagner.com glossary "taille". Status: secondary. FLAG: no date found for the change; no figure or date on card.

## Possible Atlas wording issues, raised for SITE (no Atlas file touched)
- process.html: "the law allows nothing else" for hand harvesting. The sources I saw (DGCCRF, Comité) say grapes are picked by hand so bunches arrive whole; I did not see the cahier clause itself.
- process.html: "six half-turns, always six"; sources say "traditionally".
- process.html and school.html: "Fifteen months on lees is the legal floor for NV". The cahier counts 15 months from tirage to release, with disgorgement allowed from 12 months (older text). So "15 months on lees" overstates the lees part.
