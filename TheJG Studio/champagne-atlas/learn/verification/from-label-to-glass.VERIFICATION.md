## Verification record, From Label to Glass (eps 1-8)

All of this is secondary: search excerpts (WebFetch is blocked) plus the Atlas's own data. Every
checkable specific on the cards is listed below. The web-search budget for the turn ran out near
the end of research. Two planned searches did not run: the share of NV, and the Comité's own code
definitions. Neither is on any card.

### Ep1, What Every Label Must Tell You
- **Cards 2-5. DGCCRF guide; "Champagne" clearly visible and repeated on the part of the cork in
  the neck; for bottles of 0.20 L or less, another inner part of the closure.** DGCCRF fiche
  "Champagne : connaître le langage des étiquettes"
  (https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/champagne-connaitre-le-langage-des-etiquettes),
  via excerpt. Status: secondary (official source, seen as an excerpt).
- **Cards 6-7. No obligation to print "appellation d'origine contrôlée"; UMC reasoning.** UMC
  "La définition et la législation de l'appellation" (maisons-champagne.com), plus
  francetransactions.com ("seule AOC en France..."). The cards make no "only AOC" claim. Status:
  secondary. FLAG (low): the current article in the 2022/2024 cahier was not located.
- **Card 9. Élaborateur name and the commune of its head office.** DGCCRF fiche. Status: secondary.
- **Cards 10-11. Registration number issued by the CIVC (Comité Champagne), preceded by initials
  for the professional category.** DGCCRF fiche excerpt (via a search summary). Status: secondary.
- **Cards 12-13. ABV, volume, lot, allergens, pregnancy message.** francetransactions.com; Atlas
  label.html. Status: secondary / Atlas data.
- **Card 14. Ingredients and nutrition declaration for wines made since 8 Dec 2023; the remainder
  may go behind a QR code.** Reg. (EU) 2021/2117, via wineland.co.za, ESM Magazine and the Wine
  Institute FAQ. Status: secondary.
- **Card 16. Grand cru only when 100% of the grapes come from the 17 villages.** Sibling thread
  grand-cru-premier-cru ep1 (DGCCRF 2022). Status: secondary.
- **Card 17. CRD no longer compulsory since 1 June 2019.** DGCCRF fiche (via a search summary,
  which noted that one commercial source still claims a green CRD is required). Status: secondary.

### Ep2, The Words for Sweetness
- **Cards 1-2. Dosage after dégorgement; liqueur d'expédition = wine plus sugar.** UMC "Dosage"
  page; school.html. Status: secondary.
- **Card 3. Seven EU terms.** Reg. (EU) 2019/33 Annex III Part A (legislation.gov.uk copy). Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 4. Brut nature < 3 g/L, and only if no sugar was added after the secondary fermentation.**
  2019/33 Annex III (quoted in the excerpt). Status: secondary (legal text).
- **Card 5. Pas dosé, dosage zéro.** Search summary (Champagne Club, dosage guides). Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 6. Extra brut 0-6; brut < 12.** 2019/33 Annex III (quoted); DGCCRF (brut < 12). Status:
  secondary (legal text).
- **Card 8. Extra dry 12-17, sec 17-32, demi-sec 32-50.** DGCCRF fiche (sec 17-32 quoted) and
  several secondary sources. The Annex III excerpt quoted only the first three bands. Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 9. Doux > 50.** Secondary consensus; Atlas label.html. Status: secondary.
- **Card 10. Adjacent terms: only one may be chosen.** 2019/33 Art. 47(2). Status: secondary
  (legal text).
- **Card 11. ±3 g/L tolerance against the label.** 2019/33 Art. 47(3). Status: secondary (legal
  text).
- **Card 14. 19th-century Russian market "several times sweeter than 50 g/L".** Wikipedia
  "Dosage (wine)" gives a Russian taste of 273-330 g/L and an English one of 22-66; Bolney Wine
  Estate gives 250-330. 273/50 = 5.5. Status: secondary.
- **Card 15. British drinkers led the move to drier wines.** Bolney Wine Estate; Wikipedia. Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 16. Perrier-Jouët 1846, unsweetened for London, "partly legend".** Wikipedia "Champagne";
  champagne411.com calls it legend. Status: secondary.
- **Card 17. Pommery brut, 1874 harvest, largely the house's own account.** Wikipedia "Pommery";
  Wine Spectator; Majestic. Wikipedia "Champagne" says 1876 for the designation, using the 1874
  vintage. The card names only the harvest year. Status: secondary.
- **Cards 13 and 18-19. Why extra dry is sweeter: the words were coined in a sweeter era.**
  champagne411.com says "dry" and "extra dry" appeared in the 1850s-60s, and calls the derivation
  speculative. The card frames it as "the usual explanation" and states that no single document
  settles it. Status: FLAG (an explanation, not a documented event).

### Ep3, The Small Letters Beside the Number
- **Cards 1, 3, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14. Codes NM, RM, CM, RC, SR, ND, MA and their definitions.**
  Wikipedia "Grower Champagne"; Lea & Sandeman; glassofbubbly; enviedechamp; Atlas label.html. MA:
  "Brand of Champagne owned by a third party who is not the producer, for instance supermarket
  own-brand", attributed in an excerpt to the Comité's wording. SR as "rare": one source ("very few
  of these"). Status: secondary.
- **Card 5. Most famous names are NM.** General knowledge. The 70% by volume / 80% by value figure
  is kept OFF the cards (single source). Status: general knowledge.
- **Card 9. Co-ops provide a shared press house and cellar.** General knowledge (the CM and RC
  definitions imply it). Status: general knowledge.
- **Card 15. Look for the producer's name on the back label of MA bottles.** The card is advice,
  not a legal claim. The sources disagree on whether the élaborateur must be named, so no rule is
  asserted. Status: secondary.

### Ep4, What the Year on the Label Promises
- **Card 2. A general vintage claim requires 85% of the grapes from that year.** economie.gouv.fr
  (Bercy fiche, "au moins 85 %") and the DGCCRF note on wines without a GI. Status: secondary.
- **Card 3. Champagne requires 100% for a vintage.** wine.com, wein.plus and winewithseth. All are
  secondary, and none is the cahier text; the card says so. Status: FLAG.
- **Card 9. Vintage declaration is voluntary.** wine.com and others. Status: secondary.
- **Cards 12-14. Disgorgement no earlier than 12 months after tirage; NV sale no earlier than 15
  months after tirage; vintage 36 months after tirage.** Cahier des charges AOC Champagne (2020 BO
  version on sgv-champagne.fr for the 12 months; champagne.fr 2024-03 PDF for the 15 and 36
  months), via excerpts. Status: secondary (legal text). NOTE for SITE: school.html says "Fifteen
  months on lees is the legal floor for NV". Strictly, the 15 months run from tirage and the
  minimum on lees is 12. The vintage line, "three years on lees", should strictly be "36 months
  from tirage".
- **Card 11. Autolysis gives bread or brioche notes.** General knowledge; school.html. Status:
  general knowledge.

### Ep5, Bottle Sizes and Their Names
- **Cards 1-6. Quart 20 cl, demie 37.5 cl, 75 cl, magnum 1.5 L, jéroboam 3, réhoboam 4.5,
  mathusalem 6, salmanazar 9, balthazar 12, nabuchodonosor 15, salomon 18, souverain, primat,
  melchisédech 30 L.** Comité Champagne factsheet, champagne.education
  "Factsheet-Champagne-Bottle-Sizes-EN.pdf" (2024), via excerpt. Souverain is 26.25 L in the
  factsheet but 25 L elsewhere, and primat is 27 L; the card names both and gives no volume. Status:
  secondary (official).
- **Card 3. Magnum, from the Latin for "great", not biblical.** Search summary
  (thegoodlifefrance and others). Status: secondary.
- **Card 7. A Bordeaux jeroboam differs.** champagne.education / tanners / Wikipedia excerpt.
  Status: secondary.
- **Card 8. Methuselah, the longest-lived patriarch of Genesis.** Search excerpts (969 years);
  general knowledge. Status: secondary.
- **Card 9. "Historians have been unable to find an explanation."** champagne.fr "The Champagne
  bottle" page, via excerpt. Status: secondary (official).
- **Card 10. Jeroboam first, the others in imitation, perhaps in jest.** The Historian's Hut blog;
  hedged as a suggestion. Status: secondary.
- **Cards 12-14. Sold in the bottle of second fermentation; transfer allowed for the quarter and
  for jeroboam upward; not for 75 cl or magnum.** The cahier (champagne.fr PDF) says transfer is
  allowed below 75 cl or above 150 cl, using an isobaric system. UMC says quarter bottles and
  jeroboam and above. The cards state only the ground both sources share. Status: secondary.
- **Card 15. Magnums age more slowly.** Hedged as "often said ... experience and opinion". Status:
  general knowledge.

### Ep6, Flute, Coupe or Wine Glass
- **Card 2. Marie Antoinette died in 1793.** Snopes and others (1755-1793). Status: secondary.
- **Card 4. Sèvres breast-shaped bowls (jattes tétons) for the Rambouillet dairy, delivered 1788.**
  Search summary of the coupe-history articles (theweek, food52, others). Status: secondary.
- **Cards 5-6. No evidence for the queen as the model; the coupe predates her; the same claim is
  told of Pompadour and Joséphine.** Snopes "Palace Coupe"; thesmartset. The often-quoted "England
  1663" date is kept OFF the cards: the attributions, to Ravenscroft or to a Benedictine monk,
  conflict. Status: secondary.
- **Card 10. Higher CO2 in the headspace above a flute than above a coupe.** Liger-Belair et al.,
  PLoS ONE 2012, doi 10.1371/journal.pone.0030628 (Reims); phys.org and ScienceDaily. Status:
  secondary (peer-reviewed).
- **Card 11. CO2 prickles the nose.** General knowledge, from the coverage of the same work. Status:
  general knowledge. FLAG (low).
- **Card 12. The Comité recommends a tall, bulbous glass with a narrow top, like a tulip; flute
  and coupe "aren't best suited".** champagne.fr "What sort of glass should I enjoy my Champagne
  in?", via excerpt. Status: secondary (official).
- **Card 14. Sommeliers use white wine glasses.** vinovonk, pepites-en-champagne. Status: secondary.
- **Card 15. The measurements cover gas, not aroma perception.** Search summary of the 2012
  paper's scope. Status: secondary.
- **Card 16. A tilted pour keeps more CO2.** Liger-Belair coverage (qz.com, ScienceDaily). Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 17. Detergent affects the bubbles.** Atlas serving.html; general knowledge. Status: Atlas
  data / general knowledge.

### Ep7, Myths at the Table
- **Cards 1, 3. 8-10 °C, up to 12 °C for vintages; ice and water for about 30 minutes.** Comité
  Champagne "Factsheet - Serving Champagne" (champagne.education, 2026-08), via excerpt. The Bureau
  du Champagne tip (Fine Dining Lovers) says 20 minutes with salt; the card follows the Comité.
  Status: secondary (official).
- **Card 2. Too cold numbs the aromas.** Pernod Ricard; several guides. Status: secondary.
- **Card 4. About 6 atmospheres.** Langmuir 2002, via hypertextbook.com; other sources give 5-6;
  "roughly six times" atmospheric pressure is computed. Status: secondary.
- **Card 5. Cork eye injuries.** British Journal of Ophthalmology, reported second-hand
  (peacefmonline). Status: secondary. FLAG (low).
- **Card 6. Corks at about 40 km/h at 8-10 °C, faster when warmer.** Liger-Belair (Reims), via the
  Decanter / Cosmos excerpts. Status: secondary.
- **Cards 7-8. Muselet; loosen the cage; hand on the cork; turn the bottle; ease the cork out
  slowly (UMC).** UMC "Uncorking a bottle of Champagne"; Wine Folly. Status: secondary. Kept OFF:
  the Atlas's "six half-turns, always six, on every bottle on earth". Only a retailer blog sources
  it; FLAG for SITE.
- **Cards 10-11. Hussar legend; other tales (Clicquot, St Regis); careful sources call it legend.**
  Wikipedia "Sabrage"; Fodor's; The Champagne Student. Status: secondary.
- **Card 12. A clean break at the neck with the cork inside.** General knowledge. Status: general
  knowledge.
- **Cards 15-16. 1994 study (Valade et al., Champagne / CIVC): open, teaspoon and sealed bottles;
  after 48 h, pressure in the open and teaspoon bottles dropped "by a further 50%"; sealed bottles
  livelier.** The Conversation (Geoff Scollary, 31 Dec 2021), republished on phys.org. Status:
  secondary.
- **Card 17. Stanford blind tasting, about 1994, no spoon benefit.** Search summary (Scientific
  American and others). The scientist is not named, per the brief. Wine Folly reports the opposite
  in a muddled summary; the majority view is used. Status: secondary.
- **Card 18. CO2 is more soluble in colder wine; use a stopper.** The Conversation (Scollary).
  Status: secondary.
- **Card 19. An open bottle keeps "a day or two, some sources several days".** Atlas serving.html
  says a day, often two; wine-n-gear and others say 3-5 days. The card states the range. Status:
  secondary / Atlas data.

### Ep8, Scorecard
It restates the claims above. "Names dated to the 19th century by some and the 20th by others" is
sourced to The Historian's Hut / Champagne Houses (19th) and vinovest (Champenois from the
mid-20th).

### Kept OFF the cards because sources conflicted or could not be verified
- Firm dates for the first use of "dry", "extra dry" and "brut" (1846, 1850s-60s, 1874 vs 1876).
  The house stories are hedged.
- The Atlas's "19th-century joke" line about extra dry (label.html): no source.
- The coupe's origin date and maker (England 1663, Ravenscroft, a Benedictine monk).
- When the biblical bottle names began (19th vs mid-20th century), and who coined them.
- Volumes for the melchior and the souverain (18 L vs 26.25 L vs 25 L; Taittinger 1987 story).
- The namesakes of balthazar (Magus vs Babylonian regent) and jeroboam (which king, which century).
- Whether the 37.5 cl half bottle may be transferred (cahier < 75 cl vs UMC's "quarter only").
- One number for how long an open bottle lasts (1-2 days vs 3-5 days; unverified "University of
  Reims" percentages).
- NM share of volume and value (70% / 80%), the NV share of production, and RM counts.
- The origin of sabrage; the Confrérie du Sabre d'Or (single source).
- Exact cork speed for a shaken bottle (40 km/h at 2.5 bar, Balck) and the 13-metre range.
- "Six half-turns" of the muselet on every bottle (Atlas serving.html), and the Atlas's
  "lorry-tire pressure" comparison (secondary sources say about three times a car tyre).
