# Bubble Cards — editorial standing rules

This file lives with the cards so that every session writing them reads the same
rules. Two principles, set by James on 2026-10-24, apply to every card, hook,
callback, setup, hub line and button label from here on.

## 1. Accuracy

Everything has to be accurate. Not mostly, not "close enough for a card".

- Research first, then write. Never put a checkable specific on a card from
  memory: dates, hectares, pressures, ABV, grams of sugar, village counts,
  échelle percentages, names, firsts.
- Every specific on a card has a source recorded in the verification record that
  goes in the commit message.
- Numeric comparisons, ages and durations are computed, not estimated.
- Treat every famous Champagne anecdote as marketing until it is sourced.
- When the evidence is genuinely uncertain, the card says so plainly, rather than
  picking the more quotable version.
- The Atlas's hard limits still hold (no "owner", no AOC parcel claims, no
  implied clos walls, Larmat hues are not quality, sectors are not boundaries).

## 2. Voice

Warm, welcoming, unhurried. The reader should feel seen, appreciated and welcome:
someone who enjoys a glass and is curious, not someone being tested.

Avoid the clipped, quippy register: sentence fragments for effect, punchy
one-liners, "Here's the thing.", "Spoiler:", reveals built like a punchline,
titles that sound like a magazine cover. It reads as clever at the reader's
expense, and it does not suit this site.

Instead:

- Write in full, flowing sentences that a friend would say across a table.
- Assume the reader is intelligent and simply hasn't been told yet. Never
  "most people get this wrong"; prefer "it is easy to see why this story stuck".
- Correct a myth generously: say why it was believable before saying what
  actually happened.
- Name the concept, then explain it kindly, as if it is a pleasure to share.
- Brevity still matters (around 23 words a card), but brevity comes from
  choosing one idea per card, not from chopping sentences.
- Titles describe and invite; they do not tease.

A small example of the difference:

- Clipped: "Dom Pérignon didn't invent champagne. He hated bubbles."
- Warm: "It is a lovely story that Dom Pérignon invented champagne, but the monk
  actually spent years trying to keep bubbles out of his wine."

(The example illustrates tone only; its claims are still to be verified before
any card uses them.)

## The library (confirmed 2026-10-24)

1. Grand Cru, Premier Cru
2. How Champagne Is Made (includes time on the lees, rosé, the science of the bubble)
3. The Grapes
4. From Label to Glass (sweetness, producer codes, vintage, serving myths)
5. Growers and Houses (includes prestige cuvées)
6. Champagne Understood (history, including the wars and the fight over the name)
7. Champagne and the World (rivals and climate)

Each thread: 8 episodes, the eighth always the scorecard. Each thread's spine is
approved by James in the writing window before any card is written.
